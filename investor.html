The problem

The most consequential act in an organization is the least managed.

Organizations map their processes, model their architectures, and instrument every operation with dashboards. Then they make their most consequential choices — market entries, major capital, senior appointments, reorganizations — in a room, with no structure, no record, and no way to learn from what happens next. Ask any executive three questions, and the answers sound like this:

  • Where are the decisions, including the options and reasoning behind them, recorded?Somewhere, I hope.
  • Which decisions need to be reviewed after our strategy adjustment?We need a special project for that.
  • Which decisions need to be focused on to execute on our priorities?Unclear.

This is not a failure of intelligence. It is a failure of infrastructure — and of the willingness to demand transparency and build robust, decision-driven systems in the first place. Organizations lack a system of record for management decisions the way they once lacked one for customers, processes, and architecture — until Salesforce, Signavio, and LeanIX made those objects visible and governable. The decision is the last major organizational object without a home.

The solution

A system of record for the management decision.

Arbitara combines a practitioner's decision framework with the technology to operate it — capturing, governing, and learning from every decision, so accountability and speed stop trading against each other. Each consequential decision becomes a Decision Object — a structured, persistent record that captures the full lifecycle: the framing, the options evaluated (including do-nothing), the criteria applied, the assumptions recorded with review dates, the accountable owner, and the outcome tracked over time. It is not a place to write down what was decided. It is infrastructure that makes the decision visible, queryable, and permanently connected to the strategy it serves.

Intelligence & advisory

Every decision leaves a reusable asset behind. The process becomes decision intelligence.

AI advises. The human decides. Always. Management decisions are not primarily an analytical problem. They are an organizational commitment problem, and the two require different instruments. AI expands the decision-maker's field of view; it does not carry the moral accountability the choice demands. That accountability requires someone who can be questioned, explain their reasoning, and bear the consequences of being wrong — which is what makes the platform adoptable at board level, where an autonomous "decision engine" is not.

What the intelligence layer does

Raises the quality of the human choice

  • Structures: surfaces relevant criteria and flags when the do-nothing option is missing.
  • Generates options: surfaces analogous past decisions and what happened.
  • Monitors assumptions: flags when one nears its review date or is contradicted.
  • Detects bias: makes visible the options never evaluated, the criteria overweighted.
What it must never do

Take the decision, or the accountability

  • It does not select the final criteria or set the weights.
  • It does not recommend which option to choose. The decision stays open until a human closes it.
  • It does not revise assumptions or reopen a decision on its own.
  • It does not correct the bias. It only makes it visible for a human to act on.

Why we win

Capable neighbors, an empty center.

The landscape is not empty. It's full of capable, well-capitalized platforms solving adjacent problems — none of them the one Arbitara solves. The management decision as a structured, persistent, governable object belongs to no one.

RingPlayersWhy they don't own the decision
Adjacent infrastructureSignavio · LeanIX · CelonisValidated the pattern of making invisible organizational assets visible and governable — for processes and architecture, not decisions.

BI & analyticsPower BI · Tableau · LookerAnswer what happened, with no mechanism to connect outcomes back to the decisions, options, and assumptions that created them.
AI productivityNotion AI · Microsoft Copilot · ChatGPT EnterpriseGenerate output on demand — no persistent record, no structured object, no governance layer.
Operational AI decisioningFICO · IBM · SAS · Aera · Pega · Oracle · Quantexa · ACTICO (Gartner MQ, Jan 2026)Automate computable decisions with defined rules — not the judgment calls management makes under genuine uncertainty.

The moat is time, not features. Each of these players could add a "decisions" feature — none can retroactively own the object. Arbitara's defensibility compounds: every Decision Object an organization records deepens an institutional memory that lives nowhere else, and three years of connected decisions is not a dataset a CFO authorizes migrating to a competitor. The category-creation playbook is deliberate — name the object with precision, demonstrate the cost of its absence, and build the system of record (not a tool) that becomes professionally indefensible to operate without, exactly as CRM did.

The expansion thesis. Arbitara enters through a single function — Strategy, Finance, or the C-suite office — and spreads as the decision-governance problem becomes cross-functional, because a decision made in one function references assumptions tracked in another. When a second business unit joins, it isn't buying seats to an existing tool — it's joining an institutional memory that already exists. Three years of connected Decision Objects is not data a CFO authorizes migrating to a competitor. That asymmetry is what produces NRR above 120%.

Go-to-market

A specific buyer, not an aspirational segment.

The first buyer is specific, not aspirational. The target is the Chief Strategy Officer or Head of Transformation at a $500M–$5B organization that has already invested in process or enterprise-architecture governance — Signavio, LeanIX, or equivalent — and feels the gap where decision governance should be. This buyer isn't discovering the category from scratch. They're recognizing it, which eliminates the most expensive phase of enterprise sales: educating a buyer that the problem matters.

Three conditions make this buyer tractable: adjacent budget (an existing governance/transformation line, not a new one to create), organizational authority (a top-down mandate, not bottom-up adoption), and the vocabulary (structured organizational objects and governance layers are already familiar from Signavio/LeanIX implementations).

The sales motion is direct, high-touch, and category-creating. No channel shortcut works at this stage — the sale needs someone who can articulate the problem at the governance level and close at a contract value that justifies a 12–18 month cycle. That length isn't dysfunction; it's the correct calibration for a platform requiring CEO or board-level mandate. In parallel, category-creation content is commercial infrastructure, not marketing: before a CSO can name decision governance to their board, analysts and practitioners need to have already named it.

Every company size has this problem. This round only targets one.
