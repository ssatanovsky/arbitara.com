A decision-effectiveness self-check

Where does your organization actually stand?

15 questions, answered honestly, usually reveal more than a strategy offsite. Answer for how decisions actually get made today, not how the org chart says they should. The four scored dimensions — quality, speed, yield, and effort — come from Bain & Company's decision-effectiveness research; the fifth, documentation & memory, is Arbitara's own addition, since it's the gap this site is about.

Quality

Looking back, how often has your organization made the right call on its big decisions?

How often are the options you did not choose explicitly considered and written down, not just the one you picked?

How often are decisions made by the people closest to the issue and with the right expertise, not just the most senior person in the room?

Speed

How often does your organization make consequential decisions as fast as the situation actually requires?

How often do decisions move forward without getting stuck waiting on one person or committee?

Once a decision is made, how often does it stay made, instead of being quietly reopened later?

Yield (execution)

How often does a decision get turned into action within the timeframe you intended?

How often does a significant decision have one clearly accountable owner, from the decision through to execution?

When execution falls short of a decision's intent, how often does your organization actually know why?

Effort

How often do simple, low-stakes decisions avoid getting bogged down in unnecessary process or approvals?

How often do your highest-stakes decisions get the depth of analysis they deserve — not rushed, not endlessly re-analyzed?

How often do the right people spend their time on the decisions that matter most, instead of being pulled into decisions that don't need them?

Documentation & memory

How often are the options, assumptions, and reasoning behind a decision written down somewhere others can find later?

When someone with decision authority leaves, how often does the reasoning behind their past decisions stay in the company?

How often do you know which past decisions are due for review because the assumptions behind them may no longer hold?

Three tests to place a decision, before you deliberate

Answer for the decision on your desk right now. All three are observable before the choice is made.

1 · The reversibility test

To undo this, would you need another decision of comparable authority and process weight, or just a routine adjustment within the original authority?

2 · The constraint test

Does this create binding constraints for every decision that follows, or does it operate within constraints that already exist?

3 · The asymmetry test

If this goes wrong, does the damage compound, consuming capital, attention, and trust for years, or is it contained and quickly corrected?

Before you decide: the ten-item pass

Run the ten-item pass over the decision on your desk.

0 / 10
  • Have I framed the right question, one with more than two answers?
  • Is this environment complicated or complex? Analyze, or probe?
  • Which tier is this: strategic, tactical, or operational?
  • Have I written down the assumptions, each with a review date?
  • Are there genuine alternatives, not one real option and two straw men?
  • Is the do-nothing option priced, its costs and risks made explicit?
  • Are the criteria weighted and set before gathering evidence?
  • Is there one named owner accountable for the outcome?
  • Will the reasoning travel, not just the conclusion, to those who execute?
  • Is there a review date: a moment the decision must justify itself again?
